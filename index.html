          Design rules the dry run added

          • A sender whose next step is switched off puts the contact in the Slow Burn and leaves the step field pointing at that next step. Otherwise the first person to finish A2 goes silent and nothing reports it — and without the second half, a slow-burn click would restart them at the beginning.
          • The stage sits inside every sender's enrolment as well as the step. That is the only way a click from the Slow Burn can resume a parked step: the stage changing back is itself what re-enrols the sender.
          • The marketable flag sits inside the enrolment and re-enrolment triggers too, so becoming marketable is itself the change that enrols.
          • The restart listens for any marketing-email click, through the last-click date on the record, because HubSpot will not let a marketing-email click re-enrol a workflow at all. In production the conditions sit in the self-updating list "RE - Slow burn restart due" and the listener enrols on membership of it: the first production version re-enrolled on the date itself, HubSpot dropped that re-enrolment without an error, and it was rebuilt on the list on 28 Sep. The first version of it was accepted by the API and stored an empty re-enrolment list: it would have fired once per contact, ever, and nothing would have shown that it had not worked.
          • It also asks that the contact's last engagement be older than yesterday, so it is live from the day after the park and not the same day; somebody who has never engaged is not held back by it. Without that, a contact parked by a click would satisfy the trigger the moment they were parked and restart in seconds. It was found by reading the finished workflow, not by designing it.
          • The two-day gap check runs once, when a contact enters a step, not before every email. The flow has to be a tree, so everything after a branch is duplicated behind each arm.
          • Nothing ever parks a contact on "Slow Burn" as their step. The end of Awareness points at the first Consideration step; the end of Consideration stays where it is and converts on the next click. A contact parked with nowhere to resume would match no arm of the restart and sit there for good.
          • The slow burn ends after about a year — HubSpot refuses a backward edge, so the loop is unrolled and finite — and no property records that a contact ran out. Re-parking the cohort once a year is part of running this, not an afterthought.
          • Converting on C1 hands the contact over — the sales list plus a task for their owner, or an internal notification if nobody owns them. Nothing sends in Evaluation.
          • A sender whose copy has not arrived carries no send action. The workflow can be on, and the state right, with nothing to send inside it.
          • Load wave contacts through list memberships, never one search. Search stops at 10,000 and the population is larger.
          • Merge duplicate records before wave one. Switch the old nurtures off before the first batch write.

          What the nurture is

          One nurture, Retrieval Engineering, replacing the RAG and Elasticsearch nurtures. A contact moves through three stages, Awareness, Consideration and Evaluation, by engaging, not by waiting. Each step promotes one asset with several emails a week apart. Downloading the asset earns a thank-you and moves the contact on. Two downloads, or a Contact Us form or a reply, take the contact to Consideration. Downloading the Consideration brief hands the contact to sales. After the first Consideration step, contacts with a technical job title get one deep-dive email, Radu's embedding workshop (step D1); everyone else goes straight on to the next step with nothing sent. Someone who runs out of live steps goes to a Slow Burn of useful content, and clicking any marketing email while they are there brings them back to the step they were parked on. The slow burn itself runs for about a year and then has to be re-started deliberately, which is where the archive rule decides who comes back.

          Two questions still open with Tim

          The build follows his sheet exactly as it stands. Two things on that sheet do not hold up, the simulator still reports both as failing checks, and neither is being quietly fixed here: they are his call.

          Why "stop when the step changes" is not what gets built

          Each sender needs to stop the moment a contact moves on. HubSpot in this portal has no "this field changed" test — not in any of its 64 workflows or 351 lists — so the nearest thing is used instead: stop when the step field is anything other than this step.

          That is worse in two specific ways, and it is never to be described as equivalent. If somebody empties the step field, the test asks "is it a different value" and an empty field is not a value, so the sender keeps sending to a contact who has left the nurture. And if the same step is written again, nothing notices, so a step cannot be restarted by rewriting it.

          Neither happens in the flow as designed. Both are on the list of things proved with a test contact before anything is switched on, and the practical consequence is a rule: the step field never goes into an import file.

          Words used on the map

          Sender. The workflow that sends one step's emails. It starts when the step field becomes its step, sends one email a week on send days, stops when the step field holds anything else, and advances the step by time when its emails run out. One per step. Every sender is built and every one is off until launch; at launch the senders whose copy is written go on, and the placeholder steps stay off.

          Listener. The workflow that reacts to a click in one step's emails. It marks the asset held, sends the acknowledgment straight away, then advances the contact or moves them to Consideration. Built only for the steps whose emails are real.

          Placeholder. A step whose copy has not been written gets one email saying so, and its sender stays off. The step exists, so nothing has to be rebuilt when the copy arrives — the text is swapped and the sender is switched on.

          Acknowledgment, the .9 email. Tim's convention: after a download, one short thank-you email whose button is Contact Us. Sent only to people who converted — and only where he wrote one.

          Held. The contact already has the asset. Nothing is promoted twice.

          Parked. The next step's sender is off, so the contact waits in the Slow Burn with the step field still pointing at that step. Nobody goes quiet without a state that says where they are. At the end of Awareness the field points at the first Consideration step, so there is always somewhere to resume; at the end of Consideration there is nowhere left to point, so a click from there is treated as a conversion and the contact goes to sales.

          Send window.

          Suppression list. Sales Qualified Leads, customers and contacts on open deals. On every sender, so the nurture never cuts across a live sales conversation. A branch before each send also checks the contact has not opted out; if they have, they stop in that step rather than skipping the one email, because the flow has to be a tree and its branches cannot rejoin.

          The two-day gap. When a contact enters a step, one branch asks whether another marketing email went to them in the last two days and waits two days if it did. It runs once, at that entry, for the same tree reason: everything after the branch is duplicated behind each arm, and per-email that would be sixty-four copies of A1. So the first email of every step is spaced and the rest are not. It is not a frequency cap and is not described as one.

          Batch load. The first population is written in waves by the API, warmest first. HubSpot only enrols on a change, so the write is the enrolment.
